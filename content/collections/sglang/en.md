The competitive landscape of large language model (LLM) inference frameworks is undergoing a profound transformation, and SGLang — one of the most innovative open-source projects in this space — has already made the journey from academic research to commercial operation. This report systematically traces the SGLang project's history, its core technical architecture, the background of its founding team, and its path to commercialization, offering the developer community a comprehensive and in-depth look at the project.

## SGLang Founder Ying Sheng (盛颖): From ACM Competitions to the Frontier of AI Infrastructure

As the principal initiator and commercialization driver of the SGLang project, Ying Sheng's personal journey is itself a remarkable story spanning academia and industry, open source and business. According to public records, Ying Sheng earned her undergraduate degree in computer science and technology from the ACM Honors Class at Shanghai Jiao Tong University, followed by a master's degree in computer science from Columbia University, and a PhD from the Computer Science Department at Stanford University under Professor Clark Barrett, with research spanning formal verification, machine learning systems, and large language models. This academic training across algorithms, formal methods, and AI systems laid a solid foundation for the technical direction she would later set for SGLang.

Beyond her academic career, Ying Sheng has extensive industry experience. She worked as a quantitative software engineer at Two Sigma, completed a research internship on smart contract verification at Facebook Novi, and served as a PhD researcher in residence at X (formerly Google's moonshot factory), working on AI for Code. These experiences gave her a deep understanding of the gap between academic research and industrial deployment, and provided a cross-disciplinary perspective for her later innovations in AI infrastructure. Notably, during her time as a visiting researcher at the UC Berkeley Sky Lab, she collaborated with prominent systems researchers such as Ion Stoica and Joseph E. Gonzalez — an experience that directly sparked her research interest in efficient LLM inference systems.

Between October 2024 and August 2025, Ying Sheng joined xAI and co-led the inference team; this hands-on experience at Elon Musk's AI company exposed her to the industrial-scale challenges of large AI model inference. She ultimately chose to leave xAI, however, and founded RadixArk in mid-2025 to commercialize the SGLang project — a decision that reflects her deep conviction in the value of open-source AI infrastructure. According to public reports, Ying Sheng has emphasized on multiple occasions that AI infrastructure should pursue beauty and refinement, a philosophy that runs through SGLang's technical design and community operations. Her career trajectory — from ACM competition contestant to Stanford PhD, to technical lead at xAI, and finally to CEO of RadixArk — exemplifies how a technical leader can combine academic ideals with commercial reality.

## LMSYS Org: An Incubator for the Open-Source AI Ecosystem

The SGLang project did not develop in isolation; it is deeply rooted in the unique open-source ecosystem of LMSYS (Large Model Systems Organization). LMSYS originated in 2023 as a multi-university collaboration among UC Berkeley, Stanford University, UC San Diego, Carnegie Mellon University, and Mohamed bin Zayed University of Artificial Intelligence (MBZUAI), and was formally incorporated as a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization in September 2024. This academic pedigree gives LMSYS distinctive credibility in AI research, making it an important bridge between the academic frontier and industrial practice.

LMSYS's mission is to make large AI models accessible to everyone by co-developing open-source models, datasets, systems, and evaluation tools. The organization has incubated multiple widely influential projects: FastChat has roughly 39,500 GitHub stars, the Vicuna model once generated enormous excitement in the open-source community, and Chatbot Arena (now LMArena) has become the world's most influential crowdsourced LLM evaluation platform. As one of LMSYS's flagship projects, SGLang has earned over 36,300 stars on GitHub and supports inference on more than 400,000 GPUs worldwide every day. This organizational ecosystem provides SGLang with comprehensive support across technical validation, talent reserves, and community outreach.

In terms of governance, LMSYS adopts a distinctive hybrid academic-industry model. Its roster of advisors includes renowned scholars such as Joseph E. Gonzalez and Ion Stoica of UC Berkeley and Eric P. Xing of Carnegie Mellon University, while also attracting sponsorship from NVIDIA, AMD, Google Cloud, a16z, and other institutions. This structure ensures that LMSYS projects maintain academic rigor while gaining access to industry resources. For SGLang, LMSYS provides not only legal and financial stewardship but, more importantly, neutrality and credibility — enabling the project to attract joint investment and support from competitors such as NVIDIA and AMD.

## SGLang's Technical Evolution: From Academic Paper to Production-Grade System

SGLang's technical development can be clearly divided into three phases — academic prototype, open-source iteration, and commercialization acceleration — each marking a major leap in the project's technical depth and breadth of application. The project began with the paper "SGLang: Efficient Execution of Structured Language Model Programs," published on arXiv on December 12, 2023 by researchers including Lianmin Zheng, Liangsheng Yin, and Zhiqiang Xie. The paper introduced the concept of a structured generation language for the first time, aiming to solve the lack of an efficient execution system for LLM programming at the time. The paper was subsequently accepted at NeurIPS 2024, signaling the academic community's strong endorsement of SGLang's technical approach.

On January 17, 2024, the LMSYS team officially announced the SGLang project through its blog and open-sourced the code. Even this first version demonstrated the core philosophy of front-end/back-end co-design: the back end achieves automatic KV cache reuse through RadixAttention, while the front end provides a domain-specific language (DSL) embedded in Python for controlling the generation process. Early performance tests showed that when running Llama-7B and Mixtral-8x7B on NVIDIA A10G GPUs, SGLang delivered up to 5x higher throughput than the state-of-the-art systems of the time (Guidance and vLLM), with particularly significant gains in time-to-first-token (TTFT) thanks to prefix caching hits.

Throughout 2024, SGLang went through rapid version iteration. Version v0.2 added support for serving Llama3, v0.3 introduced DeepSeek MLA optimizations, and by v0.4 the project had implemented key features including a zero-overhead scheduler, a cache-aware router, data parallel attention (DPA), and XGrammar structured generation. During this phase, SGLang gradually transformed from a research prototype into a production-deployable inference engine, supporting a broader range of models and hardware platforms.

2025 was a pivotal year for SGLang's technical maturation. The release of v0.5 marked new heights in structured generation, multimodal support, and hardware adaptation. The release not only expanded support for vision-language models (VLMs) but also achieved more efficient structured output generation through xGrammar. At the same time, SGLang began supporting the deployment of the DeepSeek model family and became the first fully open-source solution for large-scale expert parallelism deployment of DeepSeek. These advances laid the technical foundation for SGLang's explosive growth in 2026.

As of September 2026, SGLang has iterated to version v0.5.20, which has merged a cumulative 713 PRs from 237 contributors. The new release adds support for several frontier models — including GLM-5.3-Flash, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, and K2 Horizon — and introduces innovations such as unified radix tree optimization, decoding context parallelism, and the SGLang simulator. Particularly noteworthy is the unified radix tree optimization, which raised the token hit rate for DeepSeek-V4-Flash from 43.8% to 60.8% and reduced average time-to-first-token from 1.57 seconds to 1.07 seconds — a demonstration of the project's sustained investment in technical optimization.

## SGLang's Core Technical Architecture: Front-End/Back-End Co-Design

SGLang's technical architecture embodies the core philosophy of "front-end/back-end co-design," a design philosophy that sets it apart from traditional inference frameworks. Specifically, SGLang consists of a front-end structured generation language and a high-performance back-end runtime, which work in tight coordination to execute complex LLM programs efficiently. The fundamental motivation behind this architecture is the recognition that modern AI applications are no longer satisfied with simple single model calls — they require complex programmatic interaction patterns such as multi-turn dialogue, tool calling, and structured output.

On the back-end side, **RadixAttention** is unquestionably SGLang's most innovative contribution. Built on the radix tree data structure, it enables automatic KV cache reuse, eliminating redundant computation in scenarios such as multi-turn dialogue, few-shot learning, and tree-of-thought. Unlike traditional systems that discard the KV cache once a request completes, RadixAttention retains the KV caches of prompts and generated results in a radix tree, enabling cache sharing across requests through efficient prefix search, insertion, and eviction mechanisms. The system also implements a least-recently-used (LRU) eviction policy and a cache-aware scheduling algorithm: when multiple requests are waiting in the queue, requests with longer matching prefixes are prioritized, maximizing the cache hit rate. Experiments show that in typical multi-turn dialogue scenarios, RadixAttention can improve the cache hit rate by 3-5x, significantly reducing response latency.

SGLang's back end also incorporates numerous performance optimization techniques. The **zero-overhead scheduler** uses CPU/GPU overlapped execution, asynchronously preparing the next batch while the GPU executes the current one, achieving over 95% GPU utilization. This design solves the problem in traditional inference systems where CPU scheduling becomes a bottleneck and the GPU sits idle waiting. **Continuous batching** allows the system to dynamically adjust batch size at runtime, merging newly arrived requests with those already in progress to maximize throughput. In addition, SGLang supports multiple parallelism strategies — paged attention, tensor parallelism, pipeline parallelism, and expert parallelism — as well as quantization schemes including FP4, FP8, and INT4, enabling it to adapt to deployment scenarios ranging from a single GPU to large-scale distributed clusters.

On the front-end side, SGLang provides a domain-specific language (DSL) embedded in Python that lets developers write complex LLM programs in a declarative style. The DSL offers primitives such as gen, select, fork, and image, supporting advanced capabilities including control flow, parallelism, multimodal inputs, and constrained decoding. For example, developers can use the fork primitive to create multiple parallel copies of a prompt, use the gen primitive for non-blocking generation calls, or use the regex parameter to produce structured output constrained by a regular expression. The design goal of this front-end language is to replace traditional orchestration frameworks like LangChain, achieving higher execution efficiency through either interpreted execution or graph execution.

SGLang's structured generation capabilities are industry-leading and worth highlighting. Through compressed finite state machines (FSMs) and the xGrammar grammar engine, SGLang guarantees that generated text strictly conforms to constraints defined by JSON Schema, regular expressions, or context-free grammars — with no post-processing or retries required. This capability is highly valuable for enterprise applications that demand precise output formats, such as API response generation and database query construction. In SGLang v0.5.20, the project also introduced sampling mask capture and overlapped scheduling, delivering decoding throughput improvements ranging from 17% (batch=1) to 52% (batch=64) on the Qwen3-8B model.

## The Path to Commercialization: The Founding of RadixArk and the AI Inference Market Landscape

SGLang's commercialization accelerated markedly between 2025 and 2026, ultimately giving rise to RadixArk — an event that marks an important milestone in open-source AI infrastructure projects exploring business models. Between July and August 2025, Ying Sheng and core team members began discussing whether to leave their respective jobs and devote themselves full-time to building the SGLang ecosystem. After careful deliberation, the team decided to found a commercial company, RadixArk, built around SGLang, with Ying Sheng as CEO and Banghua Zhu, who comes from an NVIDIA background, as co-founder and CTO.

In January 2026, authoritative tech media outlets including TechCrunch first reported on RadixArk's funding, stating that the SGLang project would spin out as an independent company at a valuation of roughly $400 million. Then, on May 5, 2026, RadixArk officially announced the close of a $100 million seed round at a $400 million post-money valuation, led by Accel with Spark Capital co-leading. The round was one of the most significant early-stage investments in the AI infrastructure space in 2026 — not only because of its size and valuation, but also because of the caliber of its investor roster.

RadixArk's list of investors covers virtually every key participant in the AI supply chain. Beyond lead investors Accel and Spark Capital, it includes NVIDIA's venture arm NVentures, chip giants AMD and MediaTek, and prominent investment firms such as Salience Capital, A&E Investments, HOF Capital, and Walden International. Particularly noteworthy is the rare sight of NVIDIA and AMD — chip companies that normally compete with each other — jointly investing in a single startup, which speaks volumes about the strategic value of RadixArk's technology. According to the Wall Street Journal, Broadcom CEO Hock Tan also participated in the round. This cross-industry investment lineup indicates broad consensus that RadixArk's technical approach to AI inference optimization has transformative potential.

In terms of business model, RadixArk plans to build commercial services around SGLang while continuing to advance its open-source development. According to public reports, the company's business will expand from inference into model training, aiming to provide open infrastructure for frontier AI models. Notably, even though RadixArk has gone commercial, the SGLang project remains hosted under the nonprofit LMSYS organization, preserving its open-source independence. This "open core + commercial services" model resembles the commercialization paths of successful open-source projects like the Linux Foundation and MongoDB — it guarantees technological openness and community vitality while providing enterprise customers with professional technical support and service assurances.

## Ecosystem and Industry Adoption: From the Lab to 400,000 GPUs

SGLang's technical value is ultimately reflected in its broad industry adoption and massive deployment scale. According to official documentation, as of 2026 SGLang runs on more than 400,000 GPUs worldwide, processing trillions of tokens per day in production environments. This deployment scale has made SGLang one of the de facto standards in AI inference today, together with frameworks like vLLM and TensorRT-LLM forming the infrastructure layer for large model deployment.

SGLang's industry adoption spans the entire value chain from cloud service providers to end users. In cloud services, major platforms including AWS, Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure, and Oracle Cloud all support SGLang deployment. On the hardware side, NVIDIA, AMD, and Intel have not only invested in RadixArk but also actively promote SGLang across their product lines and documentation. Among Chinese tech companies, leaders such as Baidu, Alibaba, Tencent, and Ant Group have adopted SGLang for large model inference in production. Innovative companies like Cursor, LinkedIn, and xAI likewise trust SGLang's performance and stability.

SGLang is especially well suited to AI application scenarios that involve complex interaction patterns. In multi-turn dialogue systems, RadixAttention's prefix caching mechanism significantly reduces latency and improves user experience. In AI agent workflows, SGLang's DSL lets developers easily express complex logic such as tool calling, parallel reasoning, and state management. In structured output scenarios — such as JSON generation and API response formatting — SGLang's constrained decoding ensures the validity and consistency of outputs, avoiding the formatting errors common in traditional approaches. These capabilities make SGLang one of the inference engines of choice for building next-generation AI applications.

On model support, SGLang demonstrates exceptional ecosystem compatibility. The project supports mainstream language models including Llama, Qwen, DeepSeek, Kimi, GLM, GPT, Gemma, and Mistral, as well as embedding models like e5-mistral and gte and reward models like Skywork. In vision and audio, SGLang supports multimodal models such as LLaVA and Qwen-VL, along with diffusion models like WAN and Qwen-Image. Particularly noteworthy is SGLang's speed in supporting frontier models — full inference support typically arrives on the day of a model's release or within days. For example, in July 2026 SGLang and the Miles framework jointly provided day-one support for the Kimi K3 model, and in September 2026 the project delivered rapid integration for DeepSeek-V4.1.

Breadth of hardware adaptation is another important pillar of SGLang's ecosystem. The project supports not only NVIDIA's full GPU lineup — from the consumer-grade RTX 5090 to the data-center GB200/B300/H100 — but also the AMD MI355/MI300 series, Intel Xeon CPUs, Google TPUs, Huawei Ascend NPUs, and domestic Chinese hardware platforms such as Moore Threads MUSA. This cross-platform capability allows SGLang to adapt to different users' hardware environments and cost constraints, which carries significant strategic weight amid the current trend toward AI chip diversification.

## SGLang vs. vLLM: A Coopetitive Relationship Between Two Technical Paths

In the open-source LLM inference framework space, SGLang and vLLM are the two most representative projects. They compete with each other while also borrowing from one another as they evolve, jointly pushing the industry forward. Understanding their differences and respective strengths helps developers make sound technical choices for their specific application scenarios.

From a design philosophy perspective, vLLM was developed by UC Berkeley's Sky Computing Lab, and its core innovation is PagedAttention, which borrows the paging mechanism from operating systems to divide the KV cache into fixed-size blocks for dynamic memory allocation — improving memory efficiency by 3-4x and supporting higher concurrency. SGLang, by contrast, places greater emphasis on front-end/back-end co-design: it cares not only about the efficiency of a single inference call, but is dedicated to optimizing the overall execution efficiency of complex LLM programs involving multiple model calls, control flow, and structured output. This difference in design goals determines how each performs in different scenarios.

On performance benchmarks, multiple independent tests show that SGLang holds a significant advantage in multi-turn dialogue and structured output scenarios. According to Alibaba Cloud's June 2025 test data, when serving Qwen series models on a single GPU, SGLang's time-to-first-token (TTFT) was about 18% better than vLLM's, and its time-per-output-token (TPOT) was 10% to 15% better. In more complex workloads, SGLang's advantage widens further. For example, in scenarios involving multi-turn dialogue and few-shot learning, SGLang achieves up to 5x the throughput of vLLM, largely thanks to RadixAttention's prefix caching mechanism. That said, in high-concurrency single-turn inference scenarios, vLLM generally delivers more stable performance thanks to its mature memory management and scheduling.

From an ecosystem perspective, vLLM has a larger community and broader model support. vLLM has more GitHub stars than SGLang, and its API design sits closer to the Hugging Face ecosystem, lowering the barrier for developers who want to migrate existing projects quickly. SGLang, by comparison, has a steeper learning curve — developers need to learn its DSL to fully leverage the framework's advanced features. That said, SGLang's technical depth in frontier areas such as structured generation, multimodal processing, and distributed caching gives it irreplaceable advantages when building complex AI applications.

It is worth noting that the relationship between the two projects is not purely competitive. SGLang's GitHub page explicitly acknowledges the design and code reuse of projects including vLLM, Guidance, LightLLM, and FlashInfer, while vLLM introduced its Automatic Prefix Caching feature in 2024 — technically akin to SGLang's RadixAttention. This mutual borrowing has driven technical progress across the entire open-source inference framework ecosystem. In early 2026, vLLM's core team likewise chose to form a commercial company, Inferact, raising $150 million at an $800 million valuation. The successive commercialization of SGLang and vLLM signals that open-source LLM inference engines have moved from the technology exploration stage into industrial deployment, and that the AI inference market is moving toward systematization and professional governance.

## Conclusion and Outlook

SGLang's development journey offers a highly valuable case study in the evolution of open-source AI infrastructure. Technically, through innovations such as front-end/back-end co-design and RadixAttention, SGLang has successfully broken the efficiency bottlenecks in executing complex LLM programs, demonstrating clear advantages in structured generation, multi-turn dialogue, and other scenarios. Ecosystem-wise, backed by LMSYS's academic pedigree and neutrality, SGLang has built a broad collaboration network spanning academia, industry, and research, earning support from industry giants ranging from NVIDIA and AMD to Baidu and Tencent. Commercially, the founding of RadixArk and its $100 million raise mark an important attempt at the "open core + commercial services" model, offering a reference path for future open-source AI infrastructure projects.

Looking ahead, SGLang faces both opportunities and challenges. On one hand, as large model applications evolve from simple Q&A toward complex agent workflows and multimodal interaction, SGLang's technical advantages in structured generation and complex program execution will become even more pronounced. Its ability to support frontier models quickly and its broad adaptation across hardware platforms give it the potential to become a de facto standard in AI inference. On the other hand, with rapid iteration from competitors like vLLM and TensorRT-LLM, and with major cloud platforms rolling out their own inference engines, SGLang must sustain both technical innovation and community vitality to maintain its lead in a fiercely competitive market.

For the developer community, SGLang's trajectory reveals an important trend: open-source projects are evolving from pure technical contribution toward ecosystem building and commercial sustainability. The experience of Ying Sheng and her team shows that in AI infrastructure, deep academic grounding, sharp industry insight, and steadfast open-source conviction are the key ingredients of a project with lasting impact. As RadixArk expands its business from inference into training, SGLang's technical footprint is poised to grow further, providing more comprehensive infrastructure support for the next generation of AI models. However the market ultimately shakes out, SGLang's practices in technical innovation, community building, and commercial exploration will offer the entire AI industry valuable experience and inspiration.
