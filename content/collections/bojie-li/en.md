## Key Findings Summary

Bojie Li (李博杰) is one of the most representative technical experts and open-source evangelists in China's contemporary AI landscape. His career trajectory spans top-tier academic conferences, Huawei's core laboratories, the Silicon Valley startup frontier, and global open-source knowledge production. Born in 1992 in Shijiazhuang, Hebei, Li's educational path ran from Shijiazhuang No. 2 Middle School to the Special Class for the Gifted Young at the University of Science and Technology of China (USTC), and then to a joint PhD program with Microsoft Research Asia (MSRA), where he earned his doctorate in computer science — a classic pipeline for cultivating China's top technical talent. In 2019, he joined Huawei's 2012 Laboratories as one of the first cohort selected for Huawei's "Genius Youth" (天才少年) program, at the P20 rank (Technical Expert Level A), serving as the core software architect for the Lingqu UnifiedBus (UB) and leading core technology R&D on hardware-software co-design for large model training. In July 2023, Li left Huawei to found Logenic AI, and later served as Chief Scientist of Pine AI from 2024 to 2026, focusing on AI Agent products for the U.S. consumer market. His contributions to technical knowledge dissemination are especially notable: his open-source book "AI Agents in Depth: Design Principles and Engineering Practice" (《深入理解AI Agent：设计原理与工程实践》) has earned roughly 50,000 stars on GitHub, making it one of the most influential Chinese-language technical books in the field; in September 2026, its companion volume "AI Infra in Depth: Quantitative Analysis and System Design" (《深入理解AI Infra：量化分析与系统设计》) was open-sourced and surpassed 2,000 stars on its release day. He has also translated two important technical works, "Hands-On Large Language Models" (《图解大模型：生成式AI原理与实战》) and "DeepSeek Illustrated" (《图解DeepSeek技术》), and taught the "AI Agent Bootcamp" course in the Turing community. In 2026, Li drew widespread attention for publicly criticizing DeepSeek's interview process, and subsequently confirmed that he had left Pine AI to focus on foundation model research. He is currently active in the technical community as an independent researcher.

## Education and Early Career

Bojie Li's academic journey began in Shijiazhuang, Hebei Province. After completing his basic education at Shijiazhuang Zhongshan Primary School, he attended the junior and senior divisions of Shijiazhuang No. 2 Middle School. In high school, Li demonstrated exceptional talent in computing and mathematics: in November 2008, he won a first prize in Hebei Province (ranking 2nd in the province) at the National Olympiad in Informatics in Provinces (NOIP); in July 2009, he won a bronze medal (ranking 69th nationally) at the National Olympiad in Informatics (NOI); and in October of the same year, he won another provincial first prize (top 10 in the province) at the National High School Mathematics League. These competition results laid the foundation for his admission to a top university.

In 2010, Li entered the Special Class for the Gifted Young at USTC to study computer science. During his time at USTC, he was not only an academic standout but also an active builder of technical communities: from September 2010 to May 2011, he co-founded the student Gewu Zhizhi Society and served as its vice president; from May 2011 to May 2012, he co-founded the technology department of the Special Class student union and served as its first head; that same year, he also served as the first head of the technology department of the university Youth League committee's network office; and from May 2012 to May 2013, he served as president of the university's student Linux User Group. During this period, Li also helped maintain USTC Mirrors, an experience that gave him deep exposure to the mechanics of open-source software distribution infrastructure and planted the seed for his later role as an open-source knowledge evangelist. From January to July 2013, as a member of the USTC Gewu IoT startup team, he had his first taste of technology entrepreneurship in practice.

In 2014, Li joined the joint PhD program between USTC and Microsoft Research Asia (MSRA) as a jointly-trained doctoral student, researching networked systems and reconfigurable hardware under the supervision of Professor Lintao Zhang (张霖涛) and Professor Enhong Chen (陈恩红). This joint-training model allowed Li to receive both USTC's rigorous theoretical training and MSRA's cutting-edge engineering mentorship. During his five PhD years at MSRA, Li worked as a research intern in the Networking Group and later the Intelligent Cloud and Edge Group (Systems Research Group), studying under senior researchers including Dr. Kun Tan (谭焜) and contributing to several influential systems research projects. In October 2017, Li received the Microsoft Research Asia Fellowship, which is awarded to only the top 10 PhD students in the Asia-Pacific region — a mark of international recognition for his research ability. In 2019, he earned his PhD in computer science from USTC; his dissertation won both the ACM China Outstanding Doctoral Dissertation Award and the Chinese Academy of Sciences President's Excellence Award, bringing his formal academic training to a distinguished close.

## Huawei Career and Technical Accumulation

In 2019, Li joined Huawei Technologies as one of the first eight candidates selected for the "Genius Youth" program, entering the Central Software Institute of Huawei's 2012 Laboratories at the P20 rank (Technical Expert Level A). Huawei's 2012 Laboratories is the company's future-oriented advanced research division; its name comes from founder Ren Zhengfei's reflection after watching the film "2012" — that the coming information explosion would arrive like a digital flood, and Huawei had to build its own "Noah's Ark." On this top-tier research platform, Li successively served as Assistant Scientist and Deputy Chief Expert in the Computer Networks and Protocols Laboratory and the Distributed and Parallel Software Laboratory, working on applications of reconfigurable hardware in data centers.

One of Li's core contributions at Huawei was his R&D work related to the Lingqu UnifiedBus (灵衢总线, UnifiedBus, or UB for short). Lingqu UB is Huawei's self-developed next-generation data center interconnect bus technology; its name evokes the idea of a "thoroughfare connecting nine provinces," and it aims to enable the interconnection of computing power at massive scale. According to official Huawei materials, the Atlas 900 SuperPoD based on Lingqu 1.0 entered commercial deployment in March 2025. Li served as the core software architect for Lingqu UB, primarily responsible for hardware-software co-design for large model training and infrastructure optimization — work that directly supported the system software capabilities of Huawei's Ascend AI chip ecosystem. In a May 2023 interview with Zhizao Gongshe (智造公社), Li elaborated on the impact of large models on data center networks: the ballooning parameter scale of large models dramatically raises the demands placed on data center networks; for trillion-parameter models, communication time can account for 70% to 80% of the entire training cycle, as compute units must wait for communication to complete before starting the next round of computation. To address this challenge, Huawei was developing a new interconnect bus, hoping to fuse NVLink and InfiniBand technologies to improve large-scale communication performance.

Li's years at Huawei not only gave him deep engineering experience in AI infrastructure; more importantly, they instilled in him an engineering mindset of deriving system design from hardware constraints. This methodology of "deriving design from constraints" would later become the core idea of his open-source book "AI Infra in Depth." While at Huawei, he also maintained a keen eye on frontier technology trends: in the May 2023 interview, he had already pointed out that AI is not a monstrous flood destined to replace humans, but rather a wave that liberates productivity and creates new kinds of jobs — a view that reflects his deep insight into technology's social impact. In July 2023, after Huawei made four attempts to retain him, Li chose to leave and start a company, throwing himself into the emerging field of AI Agents. This marked his transformation from corporate researcher to entrepreneur and open-source evangelist.

## Entrepreneurial Journey: From Logenic AI to Pine AI

After leaving Huawei, Li's entrepreneurial trajectory evolved from Logenic AI to Pine AI. In July 2023, he founded Logenic AI, positioned as an AI Agent startup. According to 36Kr, Logenic AI's initial focus was on companion-style AI Agents — consumer-facing AI assistant products. This choice of direction reflected Li's judgment on how AI technology would reach practical adoption: he believed AI Agents were the key vehicle for AI to move from "interesting" to "useful," and that real vertical scenarios demand learning and adaptive capabilities rather than rote memorization.

In June 2024, Li joined Pine AI as an advisor, and formally became Chief Scientist in February 2025. Pine AI is an AI Agent company founded in 2024 that targets the U.S. consumer market, with a positioning that differs markedly from general-purpose AI assistants such as ChatGPT and Claude: Pine AI doesn't just answer questions — it emphasizes going out into the real world and actually getting a task done, handling low-error-tolerance execution tasks in the real world. According to official Pine AI figures, its AI assistant has helped consumers save more than $3 million on bills from providers such as AT&T, Charter, and Verizon, with a 93% success rate in bill negotiations and an average of 270 minutes saved per issue per user. A Boston Globe reporter who tried Pine AI to find a cheaper home insurance plan ended up saving about $1,000 per year. Pine AI's core team has a distinctly Chinese and AI Infra background: CEO Stanley Wei previously served as Chief Strategy Officer and Chief Operating Officer of Agora.io and as a Managing Director at Hillhouse Capital; CTO and co-founder Vincent Sun was formerly a partner and VP of Technology at Agora.io, where he long led large-scale real-time audio-video networks and AI Infra engineering systems; and U.S. technical partner Tyler Diaz is a serial entrepreneur who served as CEO of a YC W22 enterprise software automation company and also founded a low-Earth-orbit satellite communications company that launched two satellites.

However, Li's collaboration with Pine AI came to an end in July 2026. On July 6, 2026, Li publicly criticized DeepSeek's interview process on social media, drawing widespread attention. The next day, Pine AI CTO and co-founder Vincent Sun clarified that Li had already left the company, citing differences in research direction as the reason for his departure. In his own response, Li said that since the beginning of the year he had been experiencing a feeling similar to Andrej Karpathy's — that when you build models and harnesses outside a foundation model company, there are many tricks you simply don't know, and outside teams struggle to rediscover them on their own. As he exchanged more with people in the industry, his understanding of foundation models deepened, and so he decided to join a foundation model company to do research closer to the "upper bound of intelligence." He emphasized that this was not a rejection of application-layer AI or vertical models, but a natural choice for a researcher who had long worked on AI Infra and model evaluation and wanted to move closer to foundation model training and the frontier of capability.

It is worth noting that Li's career choices were not isolated personal decisions, but reflect one facet of AI talent mobility in China in 2026. From Huawei's 2012 Laboratories to Logenic AI, then to Pine AI, and finally toward foundation model research, each of Li's identity shifts was accompanied by a reassessment of where AI technology is heading. This return from the application layer to the infrastructure layer, and from engineering implementation to fundamental research, is fairly representative of China's AI startup ecosystem in 2026.

## Open-Source Books: The Twin Pillars of AI Agent and AI Infra

Li's most significant contribution to technical knowledge dissemination is the two systematic technical books he open-sourced in succession: "AI Agents in Depth: Design Principles and Engineering Practice" and "AI Infra in Depth: Quantitative Analysis and System Design." Together, these two books form a complete body of knowledge: the former focuses on application-layer development based on large models, while the latter dives into the quantitative analysis of the underlying infrastructure. They complement each other, providing the Chinese-language technical community with a systematic learning path from applications down to the underlying infrastructure.

"AI Agents in Depth: Design Principles and Engineering Practice" was open-sourced on GitHub in July 2025, with the English subtitle "AI Agents in Depth." The book is organized around the core formula "Agent = LLM + Context + Tools." It comprises 10 main chapters accompanied by 109 runnable experiments, covering topics such as an introduction to AI Agents, context engineering, user memory and knowledge bases, tools, Coding Agents and general-purpose Agents, interaction and multimodal extensions, Agent evaluation, model post-training, continuous Agent evolution, and multi-Agent collaboration. Every chapter comes with its own experiment code and reproduction instructions, so readers can deepen their understanding of the theory through hands-on practice. The book is released under the Apache License 2.0, permitting free download, distribution, and modification. Shortly after release, the book attracted wide attention: on July 20, 2025, it gained 1,734 stars in a single day and reached #1 on GitHub's global trending list; as of September 2026, it had roughly 50,000 stars and about 5,600 forks on GitHub, making it one of the most-starred Chinese-language technical book projects. The book has also been translated into 15 languages — including English, Spanish, Indonesian, Arabic, Traditional Chinese (Taiwan), Russian, Tamil, Vietnamese, Japanese, Turkish, Korean, Hungarian, Hebrew, and Portuguese (Brazil) — with the community translation work carried out by volunteers around the world.

On September 14, 2026, Li released the book's companion volume — "AI Infra in Depth: Quantitative Analysis and System Design." This book is a natural extension of his AI Agent book: in the course of interacting with readers, Li increasingly felt that to build model-based applications well, one also needs to understand the infrastructure on which such applications run. In the book's preface, he puts forward a core thesis: "The model has become the operating system of the LLM era, and AI Infra has become the computer architecture of the LLM era." Starting from hardware constraints and model architecture, the book quantitatively derives the design of LLM inference and training systems across 12 chapters, covering topics such as a first look at AI Infra, model architecture, inference and training workloads, accelerator architecture, operators and runtimes, SuperPoDs, data center networks, inference optimization, distributed inference, training systems, resource scheduling and runtime environments, and device-edge-cloud collaboration. Like the AI Agent book, it comes with a full set of computational tools and experiments: readers can use the accompanying scripts to recompute the numbers in the book, or swap in a different model and inputs to estimate resource requirements. On the day of its open-source release, the book surpassed 2,000 GitHub stars, demonstrating the technical community's high expectations for its content.

These two open-source books are innovative in the field of technical education. First, they break with the traditional publishing model for technical books — rather than relying on commercial publishers' distribution channels, they are offered free to readers worldwide directly through GitHub and GitHub Pages, dramatically lowering the barrier to knowledge access. Second, they make "runnable code" an inseparable part of the book: every experiment has explicit environment setup, execution steps, and expected outputs, so readers are not passively reading text but actively verifying and deepening their understanding through practice. Third, they adopt an open, community-built model: readers can participate in errata, translation, and content improvement by filing Issues and Pull Requests, allowing the books' content to iterate continuously. This "book as open-source project" philosophy represents a new paradigm for the production and dissemination of technical knowledge.

## Academic Contributions and Technical Thought

Before becoming an open-source evangelist and entrepreneur, Li was first a doctoral researcher with a solid academic foundation in systems. During his PhD at Microsoft Research Asia, he published multiple papers at top computer systems conferences including SIGCOMM, SOSP, NSDI, ATC, and PLDI, on topics spanning reconfigurable network hardware, data center networks, and key-value store acceleration.

One of Li's signature works from his PhD years is the ClickNP project, an FPGA-based network function acceleration platform developed jointly with Dr. Kun Tan and other collaborators. ClickNP aims to use high-level synthesis (HLS) techniques to offload network middlebox functions (such as firewalls, load balancers, and intrusion detection systems) onto FPGAs, thereby breaking through the performance bottlenecks of traditional CPU software implementations. The project was published at SIGCOMM 2016 and was Li's first top-conference paper. Building on this work, Li led the KV-Direct project, a research effort that uses FPGAs to accelerate key-value stores, published at SOSP 2017. By implementing a minimalist communication protocol stack on the FPGA, KV-Direct reduced network communication overhead to nearly the level of local memory access, dramatically improving the performance of distributed key-value stores. In addition, together with Tianyi Cui (崔天一) and other collaborators, he developed an FPGA-based HTTPS accelerator, a project that took second place globally in the Cloud & Enterprise category at the Microsoft Hackathon 2016.

Li's technical thought can be summarized as an engineering methodology of "deriving design from constraints." This methodology runs consistently through his academic research and engineering practice: at MSRA, by analyzing the on-chip resource constraints and DDR bandwidth constraints of FPGAs, he designed accelerator architectures that achieve optimal performance within hardware limits; at Huawei, based on the bandwidth and latency constraints of data center networks, he derived communication optimization strategies for large-scale model training; and in his open-source books, he distilled this methodology into a systematic analytical framework, teaching readers to start from computation, storage, communication, and dependencies, and to make order-of-magnitude estimates against hardware capacity, bandwidth, and compute power. In the preface to "AI Infra in Depth" he wrote: "The method running through this book is deriving design from constraints — first clarify the task and quality requirements, enumerate the computation, storage, communication, and dependencies, and make order-of-magnitude estimates against hardware capacity, bandwidth, and compute power." This first-principles style of analysis gives his technical content enduring value that outlasts the iteration of any specific product.

Li's judgments about the trajectory of AI technology likewise demonstrate deep insight. In May 2026, in a livestream dialogue hosted by Tencent Research Institute as part of its "AI Frontier Thinkers" series, he put forward several contrarian views: what is overestimated is the "upper bound of intelligence"; what is underestimated is "continuous learning from the environment"; voice is the most natural modality for human-computer interaction and is moving from a "secondary modality" to a "primary modality"; and "interesting" and "useful" are orthogonal dimensions — for now, do "useful" first, and "interesting" will return after AGI arrives. In June 2026, in his keynote at Flink Forward Asia 2026, he borrowed Lord Kelvin's metaphor to pose two hard problems facing AI Agents: first, how to interact with the environment in a streaming, real-time fashion; and second, how to autonomously learn from the environment, accumulating knowledge from interactive experience the way humans do. These views not only guided his own technical choices but also provided a valuable framework of thought for the broader AI practitioner community.

## Translation Work and Knowledge Dissemination

Beyond his original open-source books, Li has also made important contributions to technical translation. The two technical works he translated — "Hands-On Large Language Models" (《图解大模型：生成式AI原理与实战》) and "DeepSeek Illustrated" (《图解DeepSeek技术》) — both adopt a "full-color illustrated" format, presenting complex deep learning principles to readers in a visual way and greatly lowering the barrier to learning large model technology.

"Hands-On Large Language Models" (《图解大模型：生成式AI原理与实战》) was co-authored by Saudi technical expert Jay Alammar and Dutch data scientist Maarten Grootendorst, with Li serving as the Chinese translator; it was published by Posts & Telecom Press in May 2025. The book explains everything through illustrations, containing roughly 300 full-color figures, starting from tokens and embeddings and covering representation models, text classification, text clustering, prompt engineering, RAG, model fine-tuning, and more. In his translator's preface, Li recalled that it was reading Jay Alammar's blog post "The Illustrated Transformer" that had truly allowed him to understand the Transformer architecture back in the day, so when Turing Books approached him to translate this book, he agreed immediately. The book came recommended by numerous industry leaders, including Yuan Jinhui (袁进辉), founder of SiliconFlow; Lidong Zhou (周礼栋), Managing Director of Microsoft Research Asia; and Junyang Lin (林俊旸), head of the Qwen algorithms team at Alibaba. During the translation process, Li also used Claude 3.5 Sonnet's multimodal capabilities to help extract and translate the text in nearly 300 images — a cutting-edge practice in AI-assisted translation workflows. In addition, drawing on the book's content and his own experience, he compiled a companion resource of "200 Large Model Interview Questions," further enriching readers' learning materials.

"DeepSeek Illustrated" (《图解DeepSeek技术》) is the companion volume to "Hands-On Large Language Models," likewise co-authored by Jay Alammar and Maarten Grootendorst, jointly translated by Li and Jiaying Meng (孟佳颖), and published by Posts & Telecom Press. In an accessible, heavily illustrated style, the book dissects the underlying technology of DeepSeek: with roughly 120 exquisite full-color illustrations across 3 chapters plus an appendix, it analyzes in detail the paradigm shift of reasoning models (from "training-time compute" to "test-time compute") and explains DeepSeek's core technologies. From conception to publication the book runs only about 100 pages, yet it systematically answers the core question of "how large models actually work" — a testament to a concise, incisive style of technical writing.

In technical education, Li also taught the "AI Agent Bootcamp" course in the Turing community from August to October 2025. The course ran for 9 weeks at about 2 hours per week, leading students through systematic hands-on projects to master the full lifecycle of Agent development. Its content covered Agent architecture design, context engineering, RAG and knowledge bases, tool calling and the MCP protocol, programming and code execution, model evaluation and selection, multimodal and real-time interaction, and multi-Agent collaboration — mirroring the content of the later open-sourced "AI Agents in Depth" book. On social media, Li remarked that the bootcamp's lecture notes had "finally been organized into a book and open-sourced on GitHub," showing that his knowledge production follows a progressive path of "course practice — book compilation — open-source release."

Another hallmark of Li's knowledge dissemination is the continuous updating of his personal blog, 01.me. The blog is not only a platform for publishing his technical views but also an aggregation hub for his open-source books, courses, talks, and other content. From the Huawei interview series in 2023, to the translation announcements and bootcamp course in 2025, to the AI commentary and science fiction writing in 2026, 01.me documents the complete trajectory of Li's intellectual evolution from technical expert to knowledge evangelist. Particularly noteworthy: in March 2026, Li published an original science fiction novella on the blog, "Distillation" (《蒸馏》), set against the backdrop of the AI industry from 2025 to 2035, which explores profound themes such as model distillation, technological homogenization, and AI safety. The novella not only showcases his technical imagination but also reflects his sustained attention to the social impact of AI technology.

## 2026 Developments and Industry Impact

2026 was a year of profound turning points in Li's career, and a pivotal year in which his personal influence expanded beyond technical circles into the broader public eye. Over the course of the year, he not only completed the professional transition of leaving Pine AI and open-sourced a major technical book, but also became a focal figure in Chinese tech media coverage through a series of public events.

On July 6, 2026, Li posted simultaneously on Zhihu and X, publicly criticizing DeepSeek's interview process. According to his account, after passing DeepSeek's written test, he was left waiting for half a month before an interview was scheduled; in the second-round interview, the interviewer arrived late and, during the coding interview, accused him of "glancing at the screen on his left" to copy code, demanding that he prove he had not plagiarized, otherwise the interview could not continue. Li chose to terminate the interview, saying he felt "gravely offended." The incident quickly sparked heated discussion in Chinese tech circles, with multiple media outlets covering it. DeepSeek made no official comment on the matter, but the incident triggered broad debate about the interview processes of top AI companies, respect for talent, and technical community culture.

More dramatically, the day after the interview controversy (July 7, 2026), Web3 investor Du Jun (杜均) posted on social media to "fire back" at Li, alleging that after taking a $500,000 investment while founding his company MetaAgent, Li had gone incommunicado, and calling him "the founder with the least contractual spirit." Li responded swiftly, clarifying that the investment agreement stipulated $1.5 million but only $500,000 ever arrived; the remaining $1 million never came through, while the company's cap table had always recorded the investor's equity based on the full $1.5 million. This public spat pushed the incident further into the spotlight and gave the public a fuller picture of Li's entrepreneurial history. Notably, these controversies did not dent Li's core reputation in the technical community — the star counts of his open-source books kept growing throughout the turmoil, showing that the community's recognition of the value of his knowledge output was not shaken by personal controversy.

On September 14, 2026, Li open-sourced "AI Infra in Depth: Quantitative Analysis and System Design" — only about two months after leaving Pine AI — demonstrating that he had not paused his knowledge production during the career transition. The open-source release was covered by multiple tech media outlets, which described it as "even more hardcore than the previous book," focused on quantitatively deriving system design from hardware constraints and model architecture. Meanwhile, he continued updating his personal blog, publishing in-depth technical essays including "The Pale Blue Dot of the AI Era" and "How Many Digital Employees Can the World's Compute Power Support?", continuing to share his views on topics such as the direction of AI Agent development and the capability boundaries of foundation models.

As of September 2026, Li's status is "independent researcher": his personal profile page shows that he no longer serves as Chief Scientist of Pine AI and is instead focused on foundation model research. His influence in the technical community continues to expand: the follower count of his GitHub account bojieli is steadily growing, and the star counts of his two main open-source repositories total more than 55,000 combined; his posts on platforms such as Zhihu and X (Twitter) continue to spark discussion; and his open-source books are being adopted by more and more technical teams as internal training material. From a broader perspective, Li represents the rise of a new kind of technical knowledge producer — at once a top-tier technical expert and an active open-source contributor, at once an entrepreneur and a knowledge evangelist. This layering of multiple identities has made him an indispensable node in China's AI technology ecosystem.

## Conclusion

Li's career trajectory illustrates the characteristic evolution of a top Chinese technical talent of his generation: from gifted youth in the Special Class for the Gifted Young, to author of papers at top academic conferences, to technical expert in Huawei's core laboratories, then to Chief Scientist of a Silicon Valley startup, and finally back to the identity of independent researcher. This trajectory is not a linear ascent or descent, but a dynamic switching among roles, each switch accompanied by a recalibration of both the direction of technology and his personal interests.

In the field of technical knowledge dissemination, Li's contributions carry paradigmatic significance. He brought the production model of open-source software into the creation of technical books — version control through GitHub, content iteration through community collaboration, automated tooling to build PDF and EPUB editions, and multilingual translation to broaden the audience. "AI Agents in Depth" and "AI Infra in Depth" are not merely vessels of technical content; they are exemplary specimens of the new paradigm of "open-source knowledge production." This paradigm breaks the traditional publishers' monopoly over the dissemination of technical knowledge, making access to high-quality technical education resources no longer constrained by geography, language, or economic means.

For the developer community, Li's experience offers multiple lessons. First, a deep foundation in low-level systems (FPGA acceleration, data center networks, AI Infra) is a necessary basis for understanding upper-layer applications (AI Agents); this full-stack perspective, from the bottom layer up to the top, gives his content its distinctive systematicity and depth. Second, the dissemination of technical knowledge is as important as technical practice itself — the social value of open-sourcing a high-quality technical book may be no less than that of building a commercial product. Third, technical experts should maintain sustained attention to technology's social impact: from his rational analysis of AI unemployment anxiety in 2023 to his deep concerns about AI homogenization in his 2026 science fiction, Li has consistently held a balance between technological optimism and technological critique.

Looking ahead, Li has made clear that he will focus on foundation model research. Given his deep accumulation in both AI Infra and AI Agents, and his sustained attention to the question of the "upper bound of intelligence," his next research direction is likely to center on the capability boundaries of foundation models, inference efficiency optimization, or the exploration of new model architectures. Whatever the course of his personal career, he has already left a lasting imprint on the technical community through his open-source books — "AI Agents in Depth" and "AI Infra in Depth" will continue to serve as important learning resources for the Chinese-language technical community, and his methodology of "deriving design from constraints" will continue to shape the thinking of a new generation of systems engineers and AI developers.
