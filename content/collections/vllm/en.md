As one of the most influential open-source large language model inference engines today, vLLM's journey is not only a legend of technical innovation but also a vivid testament to the collaborative power of the open-source community. The project originated from the deep insight of several graduate students at UC Berkeley's Sky Computing Lab into LLM inference efficiency. By introducing the virtual memory paging mechanism of operating systems into attention computation, they creatively proposed PagedAttention, fundamentally solving the fragmentation problem in KV cache memory management. In just three years, vLLM grew from an academic prototype into critical infrastructure powering thousands of production clusters worldwide, widely adopted by tech giants such as Amazon, LinkedIn, Meta, and Stripe, with GitHub stars surpassing 92,400 and contributors exceeding 2,000. In January 2026, the project's core team founded Inferact, setting a funding record in the AI infrastructure space with a $150 million seed round at an $800 million valuation, marking the open-source project's formal entry into a new phase of commercialization. Meanwhile, vLLM continues to expand into frontier directions such as multimodal inference and integrated reinforcement learning training-inference, and its 2026 roadmap reveals a grand vision of evolving from a single inference engine into an AI full-lifecycle infrastructure platform. This report systematically reviews vLLM's founding team background, technical evolution, community ecosystem building, and commercialization process, providing developers and technical decision-makers with a comprehensive framework for understanding the project.

## 2. Project Origins: The Academic DNA of Berkeley's Sky Computing Lab

### 2.1 From a Lab Demo to World-Changing Infrastructure

The birth of vLLM did not stem from a grand business plan, but from a real technical pain point encountered by several Berkeley graduate students while building a large language model demo system. According to a tweet by Inferact co-founder and CTO Woosuk Kwon announcing the company's founding, when the team was building an LLM demo at UC Berkeley in 2022, they discovered that system-level problems in the inference phase were just as critical as the model itself, and GPU memory management bottlenecks severely constrained the model's actual serving capacity. This observation prompted the team to think deeply: why couldn't existing LLM inference systems efficiently utilize GPU memory? Was there a more elegant memory management solution?

The answer to this question was ultimately found in the classic theory of operating systems. The team realized that modern operating systems had already elegantly solved the fragmentation and management problems of physical memory through virtual memory and paging, while KV cache management in LLM inference faced challenges similar to operating system memory management — dynamic allocation, fragmentation, sharing requirements, and so on. Based on this insight, the team proposed PagedAttention, which divides the KV cache into fixed-size blocks and uses a block table to map logical addresses to physical addresses, thereby achieving near-zero-waste memory management. The simplicity and effectiveness of this technical idea allowed vLLM to stand out from numerous LLM inference optimization solutions and quickly gain widespread attention from both academia and industry.

### 2.2 The 2023 SOSP Paper: The Academic Foundation of PagedAttention

vLLM's formal academic debut took place in September 2023, when the team published a paper titled "Efficient Memory Management for Large Language Model Serving with PagedAttention" at ACM SOSP (Symposium on Operating Systems Principles). SOSP is one of the most prestigious academic conferences in computer systems, and publishing an AI inference optimization paper at a conference themed around operating systems and distributed systems itself demonstrates the interdisciplinary innovation of PagedAttention — it is not merely an algorithmic improvement in deep learning, but a rethinking of core computer systems problems.

The paper's co-author list reveals the initial academic team composition of the vLLM project: Woosuk Kwon, Zhuohan Li, Siyuan Zhuang, Ying Sheng, Lianmin Zheng, Cody Hao Yu, Joseph E. Gonzalez, Hao Zhang, and Ion Stoica. This team spans multiple academic levels from Ph.D. students to professors, with research directions covering machine learning systems, distributed computing, and database systems. The paper's core contribution was demonstrating that PagedAttention can improve the throughput of mainstream LLMs by 2-4x while maintaining comparable latency, with even more significant improvements in scenarios involving longer sequences, larger models, and more complex decoding algorithms. These quantitative results provided solid academic endorsement for vLLM's subsequent open-source release and industrial adoption.

### 2.3 The Early Establishment of Open-Source Philosophy

Unlike many academic projects, vLLM established an open development strategy from its very inception. Shortly after the paper was published, the team released vLLM's complete source code on GitHub under the Apache License 2.0, a business-friendly open-source license. Behind this decision lies the long-standing open-source tradition of Berkeley's Sky Computing Lab — the lab had previously successfully incubated multiple influential open-source projects such as Apache Spark and Alluxio. Another important impact of the open-source strategy was the establishment of a transparent community collaboration mechanism. ## 3. Technical Evolution: How PagedAttention Reshaped the LLM Inference Paradigm

### 3.1 The Memory Dilemma of Traditional KV Cache Management

To understand the revolutionary significance of PagedAttention, one must first analyze the memory management dilemma faced by traditional LLM inference systems. In the autoregressive generation process of large language models, each input token is mapped into three vectors — Query (Q), Key (K), and Value (V) — where the K and V vectors need to be cached for use in generating subsequent tokens. This is the so-called KV cache. The KV cache greatly improves inference efficiency — without caching, the model would need to recompute the K and V vectors of the previous 99 tokens when generating the 100th token, and the overhead of such repeated computation is unacceptable.

However, traditional KV cache management has a fundamental flaw: it requires pre-allocating a large contiguous block of physical memory for each request. This "bound notepad" style of memory management leads to severe memory waste. Specifically, memory waste comes mainly from two sources: first, internal fragmentation — the system typically allocates a contiguous block of memory capable of holding the maximum context length (e.g., 2048 tokens), but if the actual request is short (e.g., only 100 tokens), the remaining 1948 tokens of space are completely wasted; second, external fragmentation — when large numbers of requests of different lengths are processed concurrently, GPU memory gets carved into many discontiguous small chunks, and even when plenty of total free memory remains, the system may be unable to find a contiguous block large enough to satisfy a new request. According to the vLLM paper, this primitive KV cache management approach can waste up to 60%-80% of memory, meaning that most of GPU memory capacity sits idle, severely constraining the system's concurrent processing capability.

### 3.2 The Paging Revolution of PagedAttention

Although the core idea of PagedAttention is simple, its engineering implementation embodies profound systems thinking. The technology directly borrows the virtual memory and paging mechanism that modern operating systems have used for decades, transforming KV cache management from a "contiguous allocation" model to an "on-demand paging" model. Under this new paradigm, the KV cache is divided into fixed-size blocks, each of which can store the Key and Value of a fixed number of tokens (in vLLM's default implementation, one block stores the KV data of 16 tokens).

The key innovation of this paging mechanism lies in the introduction of two core components: the block table and the block manager. The block table maintains an independent mapping table for each request (sequence), recording which physical block in GPU memory each of the request's logical blocks is stored in, achieving flexible mapping from "logical page number" to "physical page location." The block manager acts as a centralized "librarian," responsible for managing all available physical blocks in GPU memory; when a request needs new space, the scheduler requests a new "loose-leaf page" from the block manager and then registers its storage address in the corresponding request's directory. Through this mechanism, physically scattered memory space is efficiently organized and utilized, fundamentally eliminating internal and external fragmentation and raising memory utilization to an astonishing 96%.

Furthermore, PagedAttention naturally supports KV cache sharing and copying. In real-world LLM applications, multiple requests often share the same prefix (such as system prompts, few-shot examples, etc.). Traditional memory management requires storing these duplicate KV data separately for each request, whereas PagedAttention, through the block table mapping mechanism, allows logical blocks of different requests to point to the same physical block, performing copy-on-write only when modification is needed, thereby further reducing memory usage. This memory-sharing capability is especially important for scenarios such as multi-turn conversations and batch inference, and is a key technical foundation for achieving high-concurrency serving.

### 3.3 From a Single Optimization to a System-Level Optimization Matrix

Although PagedAttention is vLLM's most famous technical innovation, the project's true competitiveness lies in the complete set of system-level optimizations built around it. First, **continuous batching** breaks the limitation of traditional static batching: in static batching, the system must wait for an entire batch of requests to complete before processing the next batch, causing the GPU to idle while long requests are being processed; continuous batching, by contrast, allows a new request to immediately fill the slot when one request finishes, ensuring the GPU remains under high load at all times. This mechanism is similar to "table turnover rate" optimization in restaurants, ensuring that compute resources are never left idle waiting.

Second, **speculative decoding** accelerates token generation by simultaneously predicting and verifying tokens. This technique uses a lightweight draft model to quickly generate a candidate token sequence, which the main model then verifies in parallel, keeping only the tokens that pass verification — significantly improving generation speed without sacrificing output quality. vLLM supports not only the traditional draft model approach but also n-gram matching in prompts and MLP-based speculators such as Medusa and EAGLE, providing users with flexible performance optimization options.

In addition, vLLM implements **automatic prefix caching**, which reduces the cost of context-intensive applications and improves latency by caching the KV data of shared prefixes; **chunked prefill**, which improves inter-token latency stability for interactive applications; and **structured output**, which provides high-performance output generation for applications requiring specific formats such as JSON or pydantic schemas. On the quantization front, vLLM supports multiple quantization methods and kernels including FP8, INT8, GPTQ, AWQ, AQLM, QQQ, HQQ, bitsandbytes, and GGUF, enabling efficient inference across hardware platforms; by the end of 2024, more than 20% of vLLM deployments were using quantization. The combined application of these optimization techniques has given vLLM a complete technical matrix spanning memory management, scheduling strategy, computation optimization, and output formatting, capable of meeting the demands of all kinds of complex production environments.

### 4.2 Broad Industry Adoption

vLLM's influence extends far beyond the open-source community, having penetrated the core production environments of the global AI industry. According to an in-depth analysis report published by TMTPost, vLLM's user list covers nearly half of the global AI industry: Amazon's Rufus shopping assistant and LinkedIn's AI features both run on vLLM; leading AI companies such as Meta, Mistral AI, Cohere, and IBM also use it in production; after migrating to vLLM, Stripe reduced inference costs by 73%, with 50 million API calls per day requiring only one-third of the original GPU cluster; Tesla uses an inference stack of KServe plus llm-d plus vLLM in production; and Red Hat, Google Cloud, IBM Research, NVIDIA, and CoreWeave jointly launched the llm-d project to build a Kubernetes-based distributed inference stack, with vLLM as the underlying engine.

This broad industry adoption has not only provided vLLM with rich real-world testing scenarios but has also created a positive feedback loop: the problems and needs that enterprise users encounter in production drive the project's continuous optimization, and these optimizations in turn further improve vLLM's industrial applicability. According to information released by Inferact, vLLM already supports more than 500 model architectures, runs on over 200 types of accelerators, and carries real, large-scale inference workloads worldwide. This broad model and hardware compatibility has made vLLM the "universal adapter" of AI inference, capable of supporting deployment scenarios ranging from edge devices to data centers.

### 4.3 From a Community Project to Linux Foundation Stewardship

As vLLM's influence continued to expand, the project's governance structure underwent important evolution. In July 2024, UC Berkeley contributed vLLM to the Linux Foundation, marking the project's transition from an academic research project to an open-source project under the stewardship of a neutral foundation. This transition carries important strategic significance: as one of the world's most influential open-source organizations, the Linux Foundation can provide vLLM with neutral brand endorsement, legal protection, and a governance framework, avoiding the risk of the project being controlled by a single commercial entity.

In 2025, the PyTorch Foundation further announced that vLLM had become one of its hosted projects, meaning even tighter integration between vLLM and the PyTorch ecosystem. As the world's most mainstream deep learning framework, PyTorch's official endorsement provides vLLM with powerful ecosystem support, enabling PyTorch users to adopt vLLM for model deployment more seamlessly. This foundation stewardship model both preserves vLLM's open-source community vitality and provides it with enterprise-grade governance assurance — an important prerequisite for the successful commercialization of an open-source project.

### 5.3 The Art of Balancing Open Source and Commerce

One of the most important strategic challenges facing Inferact is how to find a balance between the community governance of an open-source project and the profit needs of a commercial company. When announcing the company's founding, Woosuk Kwon pointed out that the AI industry is currently undergoing a migration from training-side problems to inference-side resource constraints and execution efficiency problems — inference demand is exploding superlinearly, the gap between models and the systems that host them is widening, and only by closing this gap can new possibilities be unlocked. This assessment accurately captures a key inflection point in AI industry development: as LLM capabilities rapidly improve and application scenarios continuously expand, inference cost is becoming the main bottleneck constraining AI adoption.

Against this backdrop, Inferact's commercialization strategy follows a clear logic: the open-source version of vLLM continues to develop as a community project, maintaining its openness, neutrality, and broad applicability; commercial products, meanwhile, provide value-added services targeting the specific needs of enterprise customers, such as more professional technical support, more complete management tools, and stricter SLA guarantees. This model has already been validated by multiple successful open-source infrastructure companies, such as Databricks (built on Apache Spark) and Confluent (built on Apache Kafka). Whether Inferact can replicate the success of these predecessors will depend on its ability to balance maintaining community vitality with achieving commercial growth.

## 6. Status in 2026 and Future Outlook

### 6.1 vLLM-Omni: The New Frontier of Multimodal Inference

In December 2025, the vLLM team launched vLLM-Omni, marking the project's major expansion from a pure-text LLM inference engine to a multimodal AI inference platform. vLLM-Omni's core innovation lies in native support for Audio-to-Audio and Video-to-Text, enabling multimodal models such as GPT-4o or Qwen2-Audio to directly take audio features as input and output audio features, without the need for the lengthy "speech-to-text -> LLM thinking -> text-to-speech" pipeline — thereby achieving ultra-low latency, making it especially suitable for building real-time interactive applications such as instant voice assistants.

In terms of technical implementation, vLLM-Omni ports PagedAttention's memory optimization techniques to visual and auditory token processing, saving approximately 40%-60% of VRAM compared to traditional HuggingFace Transformers when processing long videos or long conversation recordings. This optimization is particularly critical for edge devices with only 16GB of VRAM, freeing high-performance multimodal inference from being confined to high-end data center GPUs. In addition, vLLM-Omni extends tensor parallelism capabilities, automatically sharding huge multimodal models across multiple GPUs — especially important when handling vision models with more than 70 billion parameters. According to the Q3 2026 roadmap, vLLM-Omni has matured, with goals of productionizing real-time full-duplex models, optimizing top-priority interactive and multimodal models, and supporting streaming video generation and FastVideo integration.

### 6.2 The 2026 Roadmap: From Inference Engine to AI Full-Lifecycle Platform

vLLM's 2026 roadmap reveals a grand vision of evolving from a single inference engine into an AI full-lifecycle infrastructure platform. According to the official roadmap published on GitHub, the core work for Q3 2026 revolves around three directions: agentic workloads, ecosystem building, and open-source maintenance. On the agentic workloads front, vLLM focuses on production-grade agentic workloads and high-interactivity premium token processing, meaning vLLM must not only support traditional Q&A and generation functions but also power complex agent applications that require multi-step reasoning, tool calling, and environment interaction.

On the ecosystem building front, vLLM is building a mature ecosystem around three directions: quantization (ModelOpt and llm-compressor), speculative decoding (Speculators and TorchSpec), and reinforcement learning (vime, Prime-RL, Nemo-RL, and others). Particularly noteworthy is vLLM's active push toward deep integration with reinforcement learning (RL). The Q2 2026 RL roadmap shows that the vLLM side is undertaking systematic work to make reinforcement learning (training and inference) a first-class workload, covering multiple dimensions including training-inference consistency, runtime state switching, inference performance/efficiency optimization, and framework and workload support. The technical goals of this direction are to support RL workload integration with advanced techniques such as R3 routed replay, PD (Prefill-Decode) disaggregation, KV offloading, and flashinfer.

At the technical architecture level, vLLM is undertaking a core abstraction redesign called "Flat Model," with goals of completing the Flat Model migration for the top 20 model architectures, deprecating Model Runner V1, and improving production stability and failure-handling experience. Meanwhile, the Rust frontend and the refactored tool-calling implementation will be marked production-ready, further improving vLLM's performance and reliability. For large-scale serving, vLLM plans to achieve AgentX top-tier performance in distributed serving and KV offloading scenarios for top models, support production-grade distributed and multi-tier KV cache offloading, and introduce agent-oriented prefix caching strategies.

### 6.3 The Competitive Landscape and Sustained Leadership

Although vLLM holds a leading position in the open-source LLM inference engine space, competition is intensifying. According to an in-depth analysis published by TMTPost, SGLang, with its RadixAttention and structured generation capabilities, demonstrates stronger performance in high-concurrency MoE (Mixture of Experts) scenarios; TensorRT-LLM's compilation engine path can extract the highest raw throughput on NVIDIA hardware. These competitors each have their advantages: SGLang's performance optimization is more aggressive in specific scenarios, while TensorRT-LLM's deep optimization within the NVIDIA ecosystem is unmatched.

However, with the broadest hardware coverage, the most model architecture support, and the most mature community ecosystem, vLLM firmly holds the top spot among inference engines. According to the agenda of vLLM Conference 2026, mainstream hardware vendors including NVIDIA, AMD, Google TPU, and Intel are all actively participating in building the vLLM ecosystem, each hosting dedicated talks on vLLM optimization on their respective hardware platforms. This broad cross-hardware-platform support enables vLLM to avoid being locked into a single hardware ecosystem, providing users with maximum flexibility and choice. Even more noteworthy, as the vLLM team announces expansion into post-training workflows, and giants such as Red Hat, NVIDIA, and Google Cloud jointly advance the llm-d project, vLLM is evolving from an inference engine into AI full-lifecycle infrastructure. OpenAI co-founder John Schulman has also begun using vLLM for post-training work — itself the strongest endorsement of vLLM's technical capabilities.

vLLM's journey provides a near-perfect case study for open-source AI infrastructure projects. From a technical demo in a Berkeley lab in 2022, to academic recognition via a top-tier SOSP paper in 2023, to critical infrastructure carrying thousands of production clusters worldwide by 2026, vLLM completed the leap from academic prototype to industry standard in four years. The core driving force behind this success lies in its founding team's innovative ability to combine classic operating system theory with frontier deep learning needs — PagedAttention not only solved the memory management problem of LLM inference but also redefined the design paradigm of inference systems.

On the business model front, vLLM has achieved sustainable commercialization while maintaining community vitality through the "open-source project + commercial company" dual-engine model. Inferact's $150 million seed round and $800 million valuation mark the capital market's strong recognition of the value of open-source AI infrastructure. However, this model also faces structural challenges such as balancing open-source and commercial interests and coordinating community governance with corporate governance.

Looking ahead, vLLM's development path clearly points in three directions: first, continuous expansion of technical capabilities — from text inference to multimodal inference (vLLM-Omni), and from pure inference to training-inference integration (the RL roadmap); second, deep ecosystem building — creating a complete AI inference platform through the maturation of technologies such as quantization, speculative decoding, and distributed serving; third, continuous improvement of governance structure — maintaining the project's neutrality and openness under the stewardship of the Linux Foundation and the PyTorch Foundation. At this critical inflection point where the AI industry is shifting from a "training race" to "inference efficiency," vLLM, as the "Linux" of the inference pipeline, will see its strategic value become ever more prominent. For developers and technical decision-makers, a deep understanding of vLLM's technical principles, community ecosystem, and business logic will help better grasp the development trends and investment opportunities in the AI infrastructure space.
